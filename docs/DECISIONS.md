Vehicles with supplied damping keep their values (eROD front range and passive; eROD rear passive) | ζ = 0.3 is the typical effective mean for passenger cars and 0.8 the upper end for optimum handling (Ślaski 2011, *Transport Problems* 6(2), full text read). ζ<sub>min</sub> = 0.1 is a project assumption below the ≈ 0.2 optimum-ride value, giving the controller a soft end; weaker support (search excerpt: optimal semi-active minimum ≈ 0.2 × passive) | Accepted | 2026-10-20 |
| D23 | Plant model | `model/plant/plant.slx` is built **by hand** (no build script). Absolute coordinates with gravity and preloads (spring l0 = m<sub>s</sub>g/k<sub>s</sub>, tire R<sub>t0</sub> = (m<sub>s</sub>+m<sub>u</sub>)g/k<sub>t</sub>), so all states are zero at static equilibrium; outputs `susp_travel` and `F_tire_dyn` are taken relative to equilibrium. **Tire lift-off** modelled: tire force ≥ 0. Damper map and first-order actuator lag at the plant input. All values from `setup_params` (no numbers in blocks). Verified by `tests/matlab/TestPlant.m` | Hand-building is a learning goal of the author; the test replaces the reviewability of a script: model poles must equal the analytic quarter car for every public vehicle, plus rest, sign, current-effect and lift-off checks. Lift-off keeps the model valid on large bumps; it is inactive (linear) in normal driving | Accepted | 2026-10-22 |
| D24 | Controller structure | **One** `controller.slx` with a `mode` input, not one model per controller. Inside: `Control_law` (one subsystem per strategy, selected by a Multiport Switch on `mode`, default port → passive) → `Damping_to_current` (clip to [c<sub>min</sub>, c<sub>max</sub>], inverse damper map) → `Safety_and_limits` (saturation, rate limit, fault latch) → registered outputs (D19). Every law has the same interface: inputs `zs_dot`, `zu_dot`, output requested damping `c_req` [N·s/m]. Built with Simulink blocks only | Safety logic, requirement tests, code generation and back-to-back exist once for all strategies; strategies are written in physical damping, the damper's current map lives in one place; new strategies are drop-in slots (author's focus is control-strategy development) | Accepted | 2026-10-22 |
| D25 | Control-law parameters | c<sub>sky</sub> = c<sub>max</sub>, c<sub>gnd</sub> = c<sub>max</sub> *(PA)*, α = 0.5, ε = 0.001 m/s (in `setup_params`) | c<sub>sky</sub> = C<sub>max</sub> is common practice (SAGE review, L1; S-level); higher c<sub>sky</sub> lowers body acceleration (S). Hybrid benefit reported for 0.2 < α < 0.8 with any c<sub>sky</sub>/c<sub>gnd</sub> (Díaz-Choque 2021, S); α = 0.5 beat passive on a quarter-car rig (Goncalves 2003, S). ε derived: below ε the damper force is ≤ c<sub>max</sub>·ε ≈ 3 N (negligible); prevents 0/0 (REQ-F-05) | Accepted | 2026-10-22 |
| D26 | Model reference loop handling | `controller.slx`: "Minimize artificial algebraic loop occurrences" **on**; non-integer `mode` routed to the default (passive) port by an explicit integer check before the Multiport Switch | Without the setting, Simulink treats the referenced controller as one unit, reports an artificial algebraic loop and solves it iteratively: results deviated from an independent closed-loop reference by up to 1.3 A in `i_cmd`; with it, agreement is 1e-14 (round-off) and runs are ~4x faster. The Multiport Switch converts 1.5 to 1; D20 requires passive for any non-integer mode, and an explicit check behaves the same in generated C | Accepted | 2026-10-22 |

| D30 | Prediction model and states | `qc_prediction_model.m`: linear quarter car, states **x = [z<sub>s</sub>−z<sub>u</sub>, ż<sub>s</sub>, z<sub>u</sub>−z<sub>r</sub>, ż<sub>u</sub>, F<sub>d</sub>]**, inputs [F<sub>cmd</sub>, ż<sub>r</sub>], outputs [z̈<sub>s</sub>, F<sub>tire,dyn</sub>, susp_travel]; F<sub>d</sub> follows F<sub>cmd</sub> with the plant's lag τ (first order). **Ideal states**: z<sub>s</sub>, z<sub>u</sub>, z<sub>r</sub> (deviations from static equilibrium) and the damper force F<sub>d</sub> come from the plant as new controller inputs; ż<sub>r</sub> is formed in the controller by a backward difference of z<sub>r</sub> at the MPC rate. Shared weights and scales in `P.mbc` | Relative coordinates make every cost term a function of the state (no direct road feedthrough; an LQR would otherwise penalize the wheel for following the road). Known mismatch to the plant, accepted: no tire lift-off, lag on force instead of damping coefficient, no gravity. Estimation from sensors is a later step (author's decision: ideal states first) | Accepted | 2026-11-01 |
| D31 | Classic MPC (Law_5_MPC) | MPC Toolbox **MPC Controller block**, object `mpc_law5` from `design_mpc(P)`; formulation 1 = classic linear MPC: quadratic cost on the scaled outputs (weights `P.mbc.y_weight`), force (`u_weight`) and force change (`P.mpc.du_weight`), receding horizon. **T<sub>s,MPC</sub> = 5 ms, prediction horizon p = 10, control horizon m = 5** (`P.mpc`, changeable). MV = F<sub>cmd</sub>, MD = ż<sub>r</sub> (held over the horizon), custom state input (no Kalman filter, no disturbance states), KWIK active-set solver. Other formulations: one more case in `design_mpc` if the same block fits, otherwise their own law slot reusing D30 and D32 | Classic receding-horizon MPC is the author's main goal; p and m set by the author. 5 ms gives 50 ms of prediction (half a wheel-hop period at ≈ 10 Hz) at a QP rate 5× below the controller's. Verified in TestModelBasedDesign: the toolbox move equals the hand-written condensed problem (rel. 1e-6) and, unconstrained with a 6 s horizon, the LQR gain (rel. 1e-6) | Accepted | 2026-11-01 |
| D32 | Semi-active limit in the model-based laws | **Inside the MPC:** at every MPC step the force limits [min, max](c<sub>min</sub>·v<sub>rel</sub>, c<sub>max</sub>·v<sub>rel</sub>) (v<sub>rel</sub> frozen at that step) are given to the QP through the block's umin/umax inports, so the optimizer knows the damper can only dissipate. **After the law** (`force_to_damping.m`, block Force_to_damping): c<sub>req</sub> = F<sub>cmd</sub>/v<sub>rel</sub> if F<sub>cmd</sub>·v<sub>rel</sub> > 0 and \|v<sub>rel</sub>\| ≥ ε; otherwise, and when the MPC returns status ≤ 0, **c<sub>req</sub> = c<sub>passive</sub>**. Clipping to [c<sub>min</sub>, c<sub>max</sub>] stays in Damping_to_current. Same conversion for the LQR (no status) | Author's decision: the MPC must know the constraint, and a request that cannot be produced goes to passive (not c<sub>min</sub> as in skyhook). With the limits in the QP, F<sub>cmd</sub>/v<sub>rel</sub> already lies in [c<sub>min</sub>, c<sub>max</sub>]; the passive fallback then acts at v<sub>rel</sub> ≈ 0 and on solver failure. Freezing v<sub>rel</sub> over the 50 ms horizon is the simplification of the classic formulation (a formulation with time-varying limits is a later variant). REQ-F-06, REQ-F-07 | Accepted | 2026-11-01 |

No performance requirement of its own | Verification stepping stone: it exercises the new interface, the states and the force-to-damping logic before the MPC, and it is the limit an unconstrained long-horizon MPC must reach (oracle test). ## D16 – Scaled variant targets

Status: **Accepted** · 2026-10-20 · Method: D15.

Inputs (m<sub>s</sub>, m<sub>u</sub>/m<sub>s</sub>, f<sub>body</sub>, f<sub>wheel</sub>)
are **project assumptions** (engineering estimates per vehicle class).
k<sub>s</sub> and k<sub>t</sub> are solved so the undamped 2-DOF quarter car
has exactly the target frequencies (softer-suspension root). Each variant is
labelled "scaled variant (project assumption)", not a real vehicle.

| Variant | m<sub>s</sub> [kg] | m<sub>u</sub>/m<sub>s</sub> | f<sub>body</sub> [Hz] | f<sub>wheel</sub> [Hz] | m<sub>u</sub> [kg] | k<sub>s</sub> [N/m] | k<sub>t</sub> [N/m] | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Small car | 190 | 0.16 | 1.35 | 12.0 | 30.4 | 14 989 | 157 621 | Light body → firmer, higher body frequency; light wheels |
| Sedan (reference) | 230 | 0.15 | 1.21 | 11.0 | 34.5 | 14 559 | 150 190 | Published values (D14), not scaled; listed for comparison |
| SUV | 420 | 0.14 | 1.15 | 10.5 | 58.8 | 24 259 | 231 342 | Heavy body tuned for comfort; large wheels → lower wheel frequency |
| Light van | 480 | 0.16 | 1.45 | 11.0 | 76.8 | 45 633 | 320 305 | Stiff springs for payload; heavy axle |
| eROD front / rear | – | – | – | – | – | – | – | Author-supplied private data, local only (D21); values not published |

Solver check: solving for the reference's own frequencies reproduces its
k<sub>s</sub> within 1 % (difference from rounding f<sub>wheel</sub> to 11.0 Hz).

Caveats:
- The van is a single load state; empty vs laden differ strongly. A "van laden"
  variant can be added later as a separate entry.
- Inputs are not validated against real vehicles; D15 replaces them with
  published data when found.
- Damping (c<sub>min</sub>, c<sub>max</sub>, c<sub>passive</sub>) is not part of
  this table; set per vehicle in M2 as project assumptions.

## Notes

- **D13, changing the reference vehicle:** switching is a one-line change, but
  requirement thresholds (REQ-P) and the regression baseline were derived for
  the old reference. After a switch they must be re-derived and re-run; record
  the switch as a new decision revision.
- **D12, eROD:** parameters supplied by the author (2026-10-20); publication
  not cleared, so kept local only (D21). If publication is cleared later,
  record it as a new decision with the full thesis reference before
  committing any eROD value.
